Greece has tightened the rules for short-term rentals in recent years. For owners, that means a little more paperwork, but also a more professional market. This checklist gives an overview of the main areas to cover. It is not legal or tax advice: rules and rates change, so always confirm the current requirements with a qualified accountant or lawyer.

## 1. Registration (AMA)

Every property let for short-term stays must be registered in the short-term rental register of the Independent Authority for Public Revenue (AADE). The registration produces a number, the AMA, which must be shown on every listing: Airbnb, Booking.com, Vrbo and others.

**Check:** Is your property registered, and does the AMA appear on all active listings?

## 2. Declaring each stay

Stays must be declared to the tax authority within the required time frame, including dates and the amount received. Platforms report data too, so your declarations and their figures should match.

**Check:** Who declares each stay, and when? Is there a monthly reconciliation?

## 3. Income tax

Rental income is taxable in Greece, at rates that depend on the amount of income and on whether the activity is treated as a business. Owners with several properties may fall into different rules. Non-residents are generally taxed in Greece on Greek rental income, and may need to declare it in their country of residence as well.

**Check:** Has your accountant confirmed how your income is treated, and do you have a Greek tax number (AFM) and an online tax account?

## 4. Per-night charges

Short-term stays are subject to a per-night charge that varies by season and type of property. It is usually collected from the guest and paid to the state.

**Check:** Is the correct charge included in your pricing and paid on time?

## 5. Safety and equipment

Recent rules require short-term rental properties to meet basic safety standards, including fire extinguishers, smoke detection, a first-aid kit, emergency information for guests and appropriate liability insurance.

**Check:** Do you have the required equipment, visible instructions and an insurance policy that covers short-term letting?

## 6. Building and community rules

Some apartment buildings have rules about short-term letting in their regulations. Check the building's rules before you start, and keep good relations with neighbours.

**Check:** Have you reviewed the building's regulations, if any?

## 7. Records and documents

Keep everything in one place: title documents, registration, certificates, insurance, platform statements, invoices and tax filings. Good records make audits, sales and refinancing much easier.

**Check:** Could you find any document within five minutes?
